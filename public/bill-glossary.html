Term 1 Bill Explainer

1. What is MDI (Maximum Demand Indicator) in Electricity Bill?

Peak Power Demand Measurement, Load Sanction Auditing & Staggering Usage

MDI stands for Maximum Demand Indicator. While your total monthly electricity consumption is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh or "units"), MDI measures the highest peak electrical load (in Kilowatts - kW) drawn by your connected appliances during any continuous 15-minute or 30-minute interval throughout the billing cycle.

Key Differences: Units (kWh) vs MDI (kW):

  • 1
    Units Consumed (kWh): Measures total energy volume over the entire 30-day billing month (e.g. 350 kWh).
  • 2
    MDI (kW): Measures the instantaneous peak surge drawn at any single peak moment (e.g. 6.2 kW when multiple ACs and motors ran together).

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

On 3-phase domestic meters and commercial/industrial connection statements, MDI is printed in the meter reading table directly beside Previous Reading and Present Reading under the column labeled "MDI (kW)" or "Max Demand".

Can the Consumer Reduce MDI?

YES. You can effectively lower your MDI by staggering heavy electrical loads. Avoid turning on high-wattage appliances simultaneously (for example, run your water pump motor before turning on multiple air conditioners, or avoid operating electric irons and water geysers concurrently). Spreading power demand across different hours keeps your peak kilowatt load low and prevents load sanction penalties.

💡 Load Sanction Tip: If your recorded MDI consistently exceeds your sanctioned load (منظور شدہ لوڈ) printed on your bill, the distribution company may issue an advisory notice or assess fixed penalty charges. Staggering heavy appliances prevents exceeding your sanctioned capacity.

1. بجلی کے بل میں ایم ڈی آئی (MDI) کیا ہے؟

  • 1
    استعمال شدہ یونٹس (kWh): پورے مہینے میں کل کتنی بجلی خرچ ہوئی (مثلاً 350 یونٹس)۔

    ایم ڈی آئی (kW): کسی ایک وقت میں سب سے زیادہ کتنا لوڈ چلا (مثلاً اگر بیک وقت موٹر اور اے سی چلنے سے پیک لوڈ 6.2 کلوواٹ تک پہنچ گیا)۔

کیا صارف ایم ڈی آئی کم کر سکتا ہے؟

جی ہاں! آپ بھاری برقی آلات کے اوقات الگ الگ کر کے ایم ڈی آئی کم کر سکتے ہیں۔ زیادہ بجلی لینے والے آلات (مثلاً پانی کی موٹر، ایئر کنڈیشنر، استری اور گیزر) کو ایک ہی وقت میں نہ چلائیں۔ مختلف اوقات میں چلانے سے پیک لوڈ (kW) قابو میں رہتا ہے۔

Term 2 Bill Explainer

2. What is FPA (Fuel Price Adjustment) in Electricity Bill?

Understanding Monthly Fuel Generation Cost Differences Notified by NEPRA

FPA stands for Fuel Price Adjustment. In Pakistan, electricity is generated from a mix of sources including hydel, solar, RLNG, imported coal, domestic coal, and furnace oil. When global and domestic fuel prices fluctuate from the reference fuel price set in the base tariff, the actual cost of generation changes.

Each month, the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (NEPRA) reviews the actual fuel costs incurred by power producers and determines a positive or negative adjustment (Rs per kWh). This adjustment is added to consumers' electricity bills usually 1 to 2 months in arrears.

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

FPA is printed in the billing breakdown column under "Fuel Price Adjustment" or "F.P.A.", frequently noting the specific billing month it pertains to (for example, FPA for Dec-2024: Rs 3.20/kWh).

Can the Consumer Reduce It?

PARTIALLY. You cannot change the statutory per-unit rate determined by NEPRA. However, because FPA is billed directly on the number of units (kWh) consumed in your current cycle, reducing your total monthly units directly lowers your total FPA amount. Lifeline consumers are generally exempt or protected from heavy FPA charges.

Term 3 Bill Explainer

3. What are Fixed Charges in Electricity Bill?

Capacity & Infrastructure Charges Based on Sanctioned Load

Fixed Charges are recurring monthly fees charged to electricity connections to cover power generation capacity commitments, transmission grid maintenance, and transformer infrastructure readiness, regardless of how much electricity you consume during the month.

Under NEPRA's tariff structure, fixed charges are assessed based on consumer categories and sanctioned kilowatt capacity (kW). This ensures the electrical grid remains energized and capable of supplying power whenever appliances are turned on.

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

Listed under the charges breakdown table labeled as "Fixed Charges" or "Capacity Charges".

Can the Consumer Reduce It?

YES, via Sanctioned Load Adjustment. The fixed fee depends on your registered sanctioned load (kW) on the bill. If your actual domestic equipment demand is substantially lower than the sanctioned load recorded in DISCO records, you can submit an application at your local sub-division office to downsize your sanctioned load, which permanently lowers monthly fixed charges.

Term 4 Bill Explainer

4. What is GST (General Sales Tax) in Electricity Bill?

Federal Value-Added Tax Levied on Total Energy Charges

GST (General Sales Tax) is a federal tax collected by power distribution companies on behalf of the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR). It is applied as a percentage to the total cost of electricity (base energy charges plus fuel adjustment and applicable surcharges).

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

Under the government taxes and levies section labeled as "G.S.T.", "General Sales Tax", or "Sales Tax".

Can the Consumer Reduce It?

NO directly, as the tax rate is set by federal statute. However, because GST is a percentage of your total energy cost, reducing your electricity consumption lowers your total bill, which in turn reduces the GST amount in rupees.

Term 5 Bill Explainer

5. What is the TV Fee in Electricity Bill?

Statutory National Broadcasting Levy Collected on Electricity Connections

The TV Fee is a statutory monthly levy collected across electricity bills in Pakistan to fund national television broadcasting operations.

The standard domestic rate is Rs 35 per month for residential connections, and Rs 60 per month for commercial connections.

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

In the government taxes block under "TV Fee" or "PTV Fee".

Can the Consumer Reduce It?

GENERALLY NO for domestic consumers. However, verified places of worship (mosques, churches) and legally exempt institutions can apply at their local DISCO customer services center with documentation to have the fee permanently removed.

Term 6 Bill Explainer

6. What is Meter Rent in Electricity Bill?

Physical Meter Rental Charges for Applicable Connection Categories

Meter Rent is a nominal monthly charge levied for the physical meter apparatus installed at the premises. On modern domestic connections where the consumer paid the entire capital demand note cost for the meter during connection installation, meter rent is Rs 0.00.

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

In the charges column under "Meter Rent" or "Rent".

Can the Consumer Reduce It?

If meter rent is appearing on your bill despite having paid full capital connection fees for the meter, you can visit the sub-division revenue office with your original connection receipt or demand note to have the charge removed from your billing profile.

Term 7 Bill Explainer

7. What are Arrears (بقایا جات) in Electricity Bill?

Outstanding Unpaid Balances Carried Forward from Prior Months

Arrears (Urdu: بقایا جات) represent outstanding or unpaid charges carried forward from previous months' billing cycles into your current bill statement.

Common reasons arrears appear:
• A previous bill was not paid before the next billing cycle was generated.
• Payment was made through a bank or mobile app near or after the cutoff date and was still in clearance when the new statement was issued.
• Only partial payment was deposited previously.

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

Prominently displayed in the bill summary box under "Arrears / Previous Balance" (بقایا جات).

Can the Consumer Reduce It?

YES. If you have already paid the previous bill, take your stamped bank receipt or digital transaction ID to your local sub-division customer services office. Staff will reconcile your payment in the PITC billing system and generate an adjusted bill without arrears.

Term 8 Bill Explainer

8. What is Due Date & Late Payment Surcharge in Electricity Bill?

Payment Deadlines, NEPRA Late Fees & Installment Options

The Due Date is the final date by which your bill must be deposited at a bank or paid digitally. If payment is not recorded by this deadline, a Late Payment Surcharge (LPS) is added to your account.

Under NEPRA regulations, the late payment surcharge is generally 8% to 10% on domestic connections.

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

Bills display two clear payable amounts at the bottom: "Payable Within Due Date" and "Payable After Due Date" (which includes the late surcharge).

Can the Consumer Reduce It?

YES. Pay on or before the due date to save 100% of the late surcharge. If your bill is unusually high, you can visit your local SDO office before the due date to request approval for computerized installments without disconnection.

8. بل کی آخری تاریخ اور لیٹ پیمنٹ سرچارج کیا ہے؟

مقررہ تاریخ، نیپرا کے لیٹ فیس ضوابط اور اقساط کی سہولت

ڈیو ڈیٹ (Due Date) وہ آخری تاریخ ہوتی ہے جس تک بل بغیر کسی جرمانے کے جمع کروایا جا سکتا ہے۔ اس تاریخ کے بعد بل جمع کرانے پر لیٹ پیمنٹ سرچارج (LPS) یعنی لیٹ فیس لاگو ہو جاتی ہے۔

Term 9 Bill Explainer

9. Protected vs Unprotected Consumer in Electricity Bill

Criteria for Subsidized Rates, 200-Unit Threshold & 6-Month Rule (NEPRA S.R.O. 279(I)/2026)

In Pakistan, residential electricity tariffs established by the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (NEPRA) under S.R.O. 279(I)/2026 (effective 12 February 2026) strictly categorize domestic households into two distinct classes:

🟢 Protected Consumers (محفوظ صارفین)

Households whose monthly electricity consumption has stayed at 200 units or less every single month for the last 6 consecutive months.

• 1–100 units: Rs 10.54 / unit
• 101–200 units: Rs 13.01 / unit (with progressive previous-slab benefit)
🟠 Unprotected Consumers (غیر محفوظ صارفین)

Any domestic consumer who exceeded 200 units in even a single month over the last 6 months.

• 1–100 units: Rs 22.44 / unit (flat across all units)
• 101–200 units: Rs 28.91 / unit
• 201–300 units: Rs 33.10 / unit
• 301–400 units: Rs 36.46 / unit
• 401–500 units: Rs 38.95 / unit
• 501–600 units: Rs 40.22 / unit
• 601–700 units: Rs 41.85 / unit
• Above 700 units: Rs 47.20 / unit

Where It Appears on Your Bill:

Look at the Tariff field in the consumer information block. It will read "A-1(a) Protected" or "A-1(b) Unprotected".

Can the Consumer Regain Protected Status?

YES. If you cross 200 units, you lose protected status for 6 months. However, if you keep your consumption at 200 units or below for 6 consecutive months, the billing software automatically restores your protected status, cutting your base unit tariff by more than half!

9. پروٹیکٹڈ اور ان پروٹیکٹڈ صارفین میں کیا فرق ہے؟

200 یونٹس کی حد، 6 ماہ کا ضابطہ اور نیپرا سلیب ریٹس (مؤثر 12 فروری 2026)

نیپرا (NEPRA) کے نوٹیفکیشن S.R.O. 279(I)/2026 (مؤثر 12 فروری 2026) کے تحت گھریلو بجلی صارفین کو دو اہم کیٹیگریز میں تقسیم کیا گیا ہے:

🟢 پروٹیکٹڈ صارف (Protected Consumer)

وہ گھریلو صارفین جن کی ماہانہ کھپت گزشتہ مسلسل 6 ماہ کے دوران ہر مہینے 200 یونٹس یا اس سے کم رہی ہو۔

• 1 تا 100 یونٹ: 10.54 روپے فی یونٹ
• 101 تا 200 یونٹ: 13.01 روپے فی یونٹ (سابقہ سلیب رعایت کے ساتھ)
🟠 ان پروٹیکٹڈ صارف (Unprotected Consumer)

وہ صارف جس نے گزشتہ 6 ماہ میں کسی ایک مہینے بھی 200 سے زائد یونٹس استعمال کیے ہوں۔

• 1 تا 100 یونٹ: 22.44 روپے فی یونٹ (تمام یونٹس پر ایک ریٹ)
• 101 تا 200 یونٹ: 28.91 روپے فی یونٹ
• 201 تا 300 یونٹ: 33.10 روپے فی یونٹ
• 301 تا 400 یونٹ: 36.46 روپے فی یونٹ
• 401 تا 500 یونٹ: 38.95 روپے فی یونٹ
• 501 تا 600 یونٹ: 40.22 روپے فی یونٹ
• 601 تا 700 یونٹ: 41.85 روپے فی یونٹ
• 700 سے زائد یونٹ: 47.20 روپے فی یونٹ

یہ بل پر کہاں درج ہوتا ہے؟

بل پر صارف کے نام کے قریب Tariff والے خانے میں "A-1(a) Protected" یا "A-1(b) Unprotected" لکھا ہوتا ہے۔

کیا دوبارہ پروٹیکٹڈ بنا جا سکتا ہے؟

جی ہاں! اگر آپ مسلسل 6 ماہ تک اپنے ماہانہ یونٹس 200 یا اس سے کم رکھیں گے تو بلنگ سوفٹ ویئر خود بخود آپ کو دوبارہ پروٹیکٹڈ صارف قرار دے دے گا، جس سے بل آدھے سے بھی کم ہو جائے گا۔

10. What is Lifeline Tariff in Electricity Bill?

Special Welfare Slabs for Low-Income Households Consuming Up to 100 Units

The Lifeline Tariff is a highly subsidized social protection rate designated for low-income residential consumers with basic electrical appliances.

Verified 2026 NEPRA Lifeline Slab Rates:

  • 1
    1 to 50 Units: Flat rate of Rs 3.95 per unit.
  • 2
    51 to 100 Units: Flat rate of Rs 7.74 per unit.

Eligibility Rules:

Lifeline tariff applies strictly to single-phase residential connections whose total monthly consumption does not exceed 100 units. If monthly usage reaches 101 units or higher, the connection is billed under regular non-lifeline residential rates.

💡 Lifeline Tip: Keeping monthly usage strictly at or below 100 units (e.g. running 2–3 LED lights and 1 energy-efficient fan) ensures you retain lifeline protection.

10. بجلی کے بل میں لائف لائن ٹیرف (Lifeline Tariff) کیا ہے؟

کم آمدن والے صارفین کے لیے 100 یونٹس تک رعایتی نرخ کی تفصیل

لائف لائن ٹیرف (Lifeline Tariff) کم آمدن والے گھریلو صارفین کے لیے حکومت اور نیپرا کی طرف سے دی جانے والی خصوصی رعایتی اسکیم ہے۔

    1 تا 50 یونٹس: فلیٹ ریٹ 3.95 روپے فی یونٹ۔

11. What are Units (kWh) in Electricity Bill?

Standard Energy Measurement, Formula & How Meters Calculate Usage

In utility billing across Pakistan, one "Unit" is equivalent to 1 Kilowatt-hour (kWh). This represents 1,000 Watts of electrical power consumed continuously over 1 hour.

Examples of 1 Unit of Electricity:

  • 1
    Running a 1,000-Watt electric iron or heater continuously for 1 hour.
  • 2
    Running a modern 30-Watt BLDC ceiling fan for approximately 33 hours.
  • 3
    Running five 20-Watt LED bulbs (total 100 Watts) for 10 hours.

How Your Bill Calculates Units:

Present Meter Reading − Previous Meter Reading = Total Units Billed (kWh)

💡 Audit Tip: Always compare the present reading on your bill against the physical digital meter display outside your house to ensure meter readers recorded the numbers accurately.

11. بجلی کے بل میں یونٹس (kWh) کیا ہیں؟

بجلی کی پیمائش کی بنیادی اکائی اور یونٹس کا حساب لگانے کا طریقہ

بجلی کے بل میں ایک "یونٹ" دراصل 1 کلوواٹ آور (1 kWh) کے برابر ہوتا ہے۔ یعنی اگر 1,000 واٹ کا کوئی آلہ مسلسل 1 گھنٹہ چلے تو 1 یونٹ بجلی خرچ ہوتی ہے۔

1 یونٹ خرچ ہونے کی چند مثالیں:

  • 1
    1,000 واٹ کی استری یا ہیٹر مسلسل 1 گھنٹہ چلے۔
  • 2
    30 واٹ کا جدید BLDC پنکھا تقریباً 33 گھنٹے چلے۔
  • 3
    20 واٹ کے 5 ایل ای ڈی بلب 10 گھنٹے روشن رہیں۔

بل پر یونٹس کا حساب کیسے لگایا جاتا ہے؟

موجودہ میٹر ریڈنگ (Present) − سابقہ میٹر ریڈنگ (Previous) = کل استعمال شدہ یونٹس (kWh)
